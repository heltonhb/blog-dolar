<h2 id="key-features">What to Look for When Shopping</h2>
<p>Not all displays are created equal. When hunting for the ideal screen for remote work, you need to weigh a few crucial specs:</p>

<h3 id="panel-types">Panel Types: IPS vs. VA vs. OLED</h3>
<p>Stick to <strong>IPS panels</strong> for most office tasks. They offer fantastic color accuracy and wide viewing angles, meaning colors won't wash out if you shift in your chair. VA panels have deeper blacks (great for movie nights), while OLEDs are stunning but usually overkill (and pricey) for typing emails.</p>

<h3 id="resolution-and-size">Resolution and Size</h3>
<p>For most people, a 27-inch monitor running at 1440p (QHD) is the sweet spot. It gives you plenty of workspace without making text too tiny to read. If you love massive spreadsheets, an ultrawide display might be your new best friend.</p>

<h2 id="setup-tips">Tips for the Ultimate Home Office Ergonomics</h2>
<p>Buying a great screen is only half the battle. How you set it up matters just as much for your physical health.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Eye Level:</strong> The top of your viewing area should be at or slightly below eye level so you look straight ahead or slightly down.</li>
<li><strong>Lighting:</strong> Avoid placing your screen directly in front of a bright window or directly behind you to prevent harsh glare.</li>

<h2 id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3 id="faq-1">Is 4K necessary for a home office monitor?</h3>
<p>For most general office work like emails, writing, and spreadsheets, 1440p (QHD) is actually better than 4K on screens under 32 inches. 4K makes text too small unless you use scaling, which can reduce your usable screen space.</p>

<h3 id="faq-2">Do I need USB-C connectivity?</h3>
<p>While not strictly mandatory, USB-C is a game-changer. It lets you transmit video, data, and power to your laptop through a single cable, keeping your desk delightfully free of cable clutter.</p>

<h3 id="faq-3">Are curved displays good for productivity?</h3>
<p>Yes, especially on ultrawide monitors (34 inches and larger). The gentle curve keeps the edges of the screen at an equal distance from your eyes, making text easier to read across the entire width of the display.</p>
